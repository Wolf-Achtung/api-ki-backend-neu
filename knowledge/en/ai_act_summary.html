EU AI Act – Executive Summary

What is the EU AI Act?

The first comprehensive AI regulation worldwide. It establishes rules for AI systems based on risk levels and applies to providers, deployers, and importers of AI in the EU market.

Risk Categories

  • Unacceptable Risk (Prohibited): Social scoring, manipulative AI, real-time biometric identification in public spaces (with exceptions).
  • High Risk: AI in critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services, law enforcement. Subject to strict requirements.
  • Limited Risk: Chatbots, deepfakes, emotion recognition. Transparency obligations apply.
  • Minimal Risk: Most AI applications (spam filters, games). No specific obligations.

Key Deadlines

August 2, 2025 Prohibited AI practices ban takes effect
August 2, 2026 High-risk AI system requirements apply
August 2, 2027 Full transparency obligations for all AI systems

Key Obligations

  • Transparency: Clearly disclose AI-generated content and automated decision-making.
  • Documentation: Maintain records of AI systems, their purposes, and data used.
  • Human Oversight: Ensure humans can intervene in and override AI decisions.
  • Data Governance: Quality requirements for training data; bias prevention.
  • Risk Management: Implement systems to identify, assess, and mitigate risks.

Penalties

  • Prohibited practices: Up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover
  • High-risk violations: Up to €15 million or 3% of turnover
  • Other violations: Up to €7.5 million or 1.5% of turnover

What SMEs Should Do Now

  1. Inventory all AI systems in use (including third-party tools)
  2. Classify each system by risk level
  3. Review vendor compliance and DPAs
  4. Implement basic documentation and transparency measures
  5. Establish AI usage guidelines for employees
  6. Plan for human oversight mechanisms

Note: This is a simplified overview. For specific legal requirements, consult qualified legal counsel. Requirements may be further specified through delegated acts and guidelines.
