  <h3>Change communication at leadership level</h3>
  <p>
    Introducing AI in a mid‑sized company requires strategic communication at the leadership level. Without active involvement of decision makers, even successful pilot projects fail due to lack of support for scaling.
  </p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Conduct stakeholder mapping:</strong> Identify all relevant decision makers and influencers: management, area managers, works council (if any), IT management. Understand their perspectives: who sees opportunities, who has concerns? Adapt your communication accordingly.</li>
    <li><strong>Communicate opportunities & risks transparently:</strong> Present a balanced analysis: Which concrete efficiency gains are realistic? What risks (data protection, quality, dependencies) exist and how will they be addressed? Honest communication builds trust and avoids later disappointment.</li>
    <li><strong>Involve co‑determination:</strong> If a works council exists, bring it on board early. Clarify together: What data is processed? Are there impacts on jobs or content? Proactive involvement prevents resistance and delays.</li>
    <li><strong>Regular status updates:</strong> Establish a monthly short report to management: pilot progress, milestones reached, measured effects, next steps. No surprises – continuous transparency is key to sustainable management support.</li>
    <li><strong>Use success stories:</strong> Document concrete successes from the pilot area with measurable numbers. These proof points are your best argument for the rollout decision.</li>
  </ul>

  <h3>KPI framework for departments</h3>

        <li><em>Time savings per process:</em> Compare processing time with and without AI support (goal: 20–35% reduction)</li>

    </li>

        <li><em>Error rate:</em> Number of corrections or complaints per 100 AI‑supported processes</li>

    </li>
